Subject: An Unusual Wedding Invitation

Serj,

I’m getting married in Albuquerque, New Mexico, on October 11, and I have a somewhat unusual invitation for you.

I’ve been a fan of your work for a long time—not only System of a Down and your solo work, but your visual art and writing as well. I have a thoroughly worn-out copy of *Cool Gardens* that I’ve held onto for years, and your poetry and sketches in it have meant a lot to me.

So naturally, when I started planning my wedding, I decided the reasonable thing to do was build an unnecessarily elaborate little universe around such inspirations.

One part of that universe is the **Groomsmen Gauntlet**.

There will be a group of men who have volunteered to challenge me in racquetball. The important detail is that they are not expected to win.

**The Groomsmen lose to the Bridegroom. Epically.**

I’m inviting a handful of people whose work, lives, or art have meant something to me to participate in the wedding in whatever way feels appropriate—including people I have absolutely no reasonable expectation of hearing from.

And there is a particularly ridiculous possibility hiding in this one.

Darroh Sudderth is also being invited.

I don't need to explain that any further. If the universe somehow decided that Darroh and Serj should both end up at the same Albuquerque wedding, I think the appropriate solution would be to put racquets in their hands and make them both lose to the Bridegroom.

The invitation is sincere. The ridiculousness is intentional.

There is also a larger project surrounding the wedding called **WedHack26**. I’m treating the wedding partly as a community creative experiment: people can contribute music, art, technology, games, stories, performances, or whatever else they want to bring into the world.

There is a charitable component as well. Rather than turning the wedding's various games and projects into transactions, I’m using them to direct attention toward a community home fund and charitable giving. The goal is to make the whole thing about people contributing something meaningful—not simply spending money on a wedding.

I’ve put the project in an open GitHub space so that anyone who wants to look around—or potentially contribute something of their own—has somewhere to do it:

There is absolutely no expectation attached to this invitation. A message, a piece of art, a recording, a contribution to the project, showing up to cause some beautiful chaos, or simply reading this email would all be more than enough.

I just wanted to make the invitation.

Thank you for the music, the art, the writing, and the strange paths they have opened for people like me.

Alex
